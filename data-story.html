Output 01 · Data story

Half the world is under 30. Who is in the room?

Three published numbers, drawn as dots you can count. Built to read in ten seconds on a phone, with the source on every card.

GoalShow I can turn policy data into one clear visual for a young audience.
AudienceYoung followers on LinkedIn and Instagram; UN Youth Office and UNOPS comms teams.
Skills shownData storytelling, visual identity, accessible design.
The story

Who is affected, who is heard, who now has a seat

One idea per card. Each dot is 1% (or, on the third card, one advisor). Tap or scroll a card to see it fill.

1 · Who is affected
88%

Almost 9 in 10 young people (aged 15–24) live in developing countries, where climate and development needs are greatest.

Developing countriesOther countries
Source: FAO, citing UN data
2 · Who is heard
2.8%

of the world’s parliamentarians are 30 or under, unchanged since 2023. More than a third of chambers have none.

MPs aged 30 and underAll other MPs
Source: IPU, Youth participation in national parliaments 2025
3 · Who now has a seat
30

advisors, all under 30, sit on the UNOPS Youth Engagement Platform and advise its leadership on climate action, resilience and development.

One advisor under 30
Source: UNOPS, Youth at the table

The gap is wide, and so is the response. Half the world is under 30, most of them in developing countries, yet under 3% of MPs are. Advisory seats and the UN Youth Office’s Youth Compass are two ways to narrow it.

Design notes

One look, built to be reused

Countable shapes

One dot, one unit. No 3D, no decoration. A reader can verify the number by looking.

Amber marks people

Navy gives context, amber or green highlights the group the story is about.

Readable by all

Every chart has alt text, a text legend and a visible number, so colour is never the only cue.

Sourced on the card

The source sits on the card, so a screenshot stays honest when it travels.

Palette (click to copy)

Montserrat ExtraBold

for headlines, with DM Sans for text, labels and sources.

Reuse

Same data, four places

The same facts run through every sample, so the whole package tells one story.
